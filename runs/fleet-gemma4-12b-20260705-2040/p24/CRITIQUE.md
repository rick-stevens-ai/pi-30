1. Clock Misuse / Precision Loss: The implementation updates `self.last_update = now` on every call to `allow()`. In high-frequency request scenarios, `elapsed` (now - last_update) can be extremely small. Due to floating-point precision limits, the addition `self.tokens + elapsed * self.rate` might result in no change to `self.tokens` if the increment is smaller than the machine epsilon for the current value of `self.tokens`. This leads to "token starvation" where requests are rejected even though time has passed. A more robust approach is to only update `last_update` by the amount of time actually accounted for in the refill calculation, or use a larger time unit.

2. Clock Jump Handling: The use of `max(0, now - self.last_update)` prevents negative refills during backward clock jumps (e.g., NTP syncs), but it effectively "freezes" the bucket's state until the clock moves forward enough to compensate for the jump. While this prevents errors, it might not be the desired behavior in all systems.

3. Burst vs Rate: The `RateLimiter` implementation forces `capacity = rate`. This means a user cannot specify a lower burst capacity than the sustained rate (e.g., 10 requests/sec with a max burst of 2). While this is a design choice, it limits the flexibility of the limiter.
